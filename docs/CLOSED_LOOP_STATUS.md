# Closed-loop executor checkpoint

The offline executor and onboard mailbox are implemented as a **gated prototype**, not a hardware-ready closed-loop controller. Shadow remains the default. The bridge reports `semantic_execution_enabled: false` even if intent admission is configured on; the moving-eyes runner refuses supervised mode in that state. Admitted semantic motion is rejected by an additional onboard interlock. The **291-test** offline suite passed on November 7, 2026. Live bridge source `0.20.9-steering-sweep` was deployed and read back; separate guarded local-mission trials below did command actual motor packets, not semantic executor motion.

Two independent reviews exposed safety issues. Host-side transport checks and onboard executor-generation fencing are now implemented and exercised with fake I/O, including cancellation races; these do **not** establish that a TCP write already in progress can be revoked after cancellation. The semantic execution interlock stays closed until that limitation is resolved and independently reviewed. Visual `look` is not treated as permission for a translating curve. Stationary look remains unsupported. Uncertain delivery that expires absent from the observed mailbox is recorded as unconfirmed, not successful.

On November 7, 2026, direct DOGZILLA_WIFI maintenance isolated the live bottleneck: `/scan` had one real publisher in **ROS_DOMAIN_ID=16** and Cartographer reported approximately 10 Hz. The earlier zero-publisher result came from querying the wrong domain (0). A corrupted 9312×3293-cell occupancy grid held the bridge at about 97% CPU and made its scan callback stale despite a healthy LiDAR. With the occupancy publisher stopped and bridge restarted, the bridge counted 40 scans in four seconds. The new bridge rejects maps above one million cells before copying/counting them and unsubscribes that map feed, leaving map-dependent motion fail-closed. After deploying `0.20.6-map-load-shed`, restarting Cartographer and the occupancy publisher cleared the corrupt map, and a bridge restart reset its old pose anchor. The independently reviewed `0.20.7-map-validation` hardening was deployed afterward. A further six live samples on `0.20.7` showed 20 new scans per two seconds, a fresh 98×124 grid and `slam.usable=true`; `/slam_map` returned a valid grid, and `/frame.jpg` returned a 640×480 JPEG. The frame shows a dim low-angle room with nearby foreground clutter; it does not prove a changed viewpoint. BEEP stayed stopped, with no mission or lease. This was **sensor recovery, before the later live motion trials**. A direct V4L2 capture previously produced one decodable 640×480 room frame while the app camera was inactive; later the vendor app process held `/dev/video0` and the bridge's `/frame.jpg` route succeeded. The object named in the handoff was a replaceable perception target, not the mission objective.

Live guarded trials on November 7: inspection of the installed vendor app (`app_dogzilla.py`) showed it maps joystick percent to SDK speed as `int(percent / 100 * 0.2 * g_step_control)`, with default `g_step_control=50`. The bridge had sent SDK step 10 as **10%**, silently commanding speed **1**. `app_joystick_percent` now sends **100%** for step 10. The first five-second local trial on `0.20.8` advanced guarded Cartographer x from approximately 0 to 0.15 m before a collision veto; the front range decreased about 0.16 m and rear range increased. A second trial stopped immediately because asymmetrical side clearance requested a curve while rear clearance was 0.444 m, just below its own 0.45 m guard. `0.20.9` now chooses straight gait when the frontal corridor is open or the sweep is unavailable. A third 12-second local trial actually dispatched `forward` and kept its gait across six windows; the guarded pose advanced x from 0.146 m to 0.460 m in 3.77 seconds, front range decreased about 0.30 m, and rear range increased about 0.28 m. It stopped with `heading_drift_during_backoff` after a guarded reassessment/backoff attempt. Final `/mission` readback: `failed`, `moving=false`, no lease, fresh scans, usable SLAM. The coupled pose/range changes are evidence of translational motion. A short straight run does not have to visibly change the room view; near-identical camera panels here do **not** establish a camera fault or invalidate LiDAR-guided exploration.

Architecture: the onboard mission owns continuous LiDAR/map-based gait and local corrections, while a separate camera capture thread and Gemini inference run concurrently. The model receives a chronological panel of nonredundant camera views plus current map/pose/range context and contributes higher-level scene understanding; it is not a motor-control clock. Sensor/ownership guards, not slow LLM contemplation or repeated-looking frames, determine when motion stops. The new `scripts/run_beep_explore.py` launcher starts a bounded coverage mission and parallel shadow moving-eyes observer and cancels only the exact owned mission at its end. This concurrent launcher has been exercised against a local fake HTTP bridge; it has not been run on BEEP since the robot became unreachable. Semantic motor-intent dispatch remains disabled.
